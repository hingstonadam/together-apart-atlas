Chapter 02

Research

Research method and evidence

My research brings together entries drawn from previous projects, a site visit, conversations with practitioners, surveys, journal articles, videos, podcasts and books. These sources helped me explore how people occupy space through physical, psychological and philosophical perspectives, with a particular interest in domestic life in Australia.

The research began with The Civic Skin and the idea of an occupiable building envelope. It developed into a broader investigation of how apartments could better support everyday life, then focused on downsizing and multigenerational living. Throughout this process, I considered how spaces accommodate different routines, expectations of privacy and choices about spending time with others.

The database records this development and brings different sources into conversation. Reading across built projects, research and accounts of everyday experience helped me develop the project’s position and identify questions to explore through design.

Using precedents as questions for the design

My precedent research focused on single-family and multifamily dwellings, looking closely at their spatial arrangements and how privacy is negotiated within the home. I considered how bedrooms, living areas, entrances and outdoor spaces relate to one another, and how these relationships allow people to share a home while maintaining their own routines.

These studies helped me frame questions to explore within an apartment setting. How can related households live close together and retain independence? How can several generations share a dwelling with space for privacy? I explored these questions through the vertical, horizontal and joint arrangements, considering how connections between homes and spaces within them could support different ways of living.

This also informed my thinking about the occupiable envelope. Balconies, terraces and entrance spaces extend the places available for everyday activities, creating opportunities to spend time together or find space away from shared living areas.

From the research database

Looking at how people live close together

I use these photos to look more closely at doors, shared routes and outdoor spaces. The colour highlights pick out the parts I’m interested in and the questions I’m taking into my own design.

Research point 091 / DEFINE

Charles House

01 — A doorway between the rooms02 — A glimpse of the garden

I'm interested in how the openings link these rooms without making them feel like one large space. There's a view through to the garden, but each room still has an edge of its own. It's a useful way to think about sharing a home while finding a little space for yourself.

Research point 092 / MEDIATE

Mahogany House

01 — Walking beside the pool02 — Room to sit together

The table in the courtyard gives the spaces around it somewhere to come together. I like that the outdoor area feels connected to the rooms on either side, with space to sit as well as move through. It makes me think about what a shared terrace could offer two households.

Research point 093 / DEFINE

3 Generation House

01 — Moving between levels02 — Everyday life beside the stair

The stair is right beside the kitchen and dining table, so moving between levels is part of the life of the home. For my stacked apartments, the question is how to make that family connection feel easy while keeping each household's own way in.

Research point 094 / EXTEND

Veil + Co. House

01 — A sheltered walk between spaces02 — A door along the way

This covered route gives the walk between spaces a sense of shelter. The openings along it let someone step inside or keep moving. I'm interested in how a gallery could offer that same feeling of arriving home, with a clear route past the places where people might stop.

Research point 095 / MEDIATE

La Borda

01 — Passing by the courtyard02 — A front door of your own

The separate doors are easy to pick out, even though they face the same courtyard. Plants and small things along the galleries suggest ways people can make the edge of a home their own. I keep coming back to how these spaces can leave room to pause without taking over the shared route.

Research point 096 / DEFINE

Marmalade Lane

01 — A shared way home02 — Somewhere to stop for a moment

The lane feels like more than a way to get from one door to another. The low walls, planting and places to sit give it an everyday domestic scale. I'm interested in how that feeling might carry into an apartment building, where a shared walkway can still feel close to home.
