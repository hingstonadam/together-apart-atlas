Chapter 02

Research

Research method and evidence

The project combines precedent analysis, reading, Learning Journal studies and iterative design work. The research database records sources and interpretations, while the journal traces emerging questions through sketches and notes. Precedents are examined through their plans, photographs and published descriptions, with attention to entrances, circulation, outdoor territory and the boundaries between households. BETA’s 3 Generation House provides a reference for vertically related homes and changing mobility; Veil + Co. House informs the investigation of sheltered connections; and Marmalade Lane offers a comparison between individual homes and shared spaces.

I interpret these sources through drawing, testing how their spatial relationships might apply within an apartment building at Southwark Grounds. DEFINE, EXTEND and MEDIATE organise the investigation around domestic boundaries, outdoor occupation and choices about contact. The resulting plans and sections allow these relationships to be examined, but do not establish residents’ experiences or social outcomes. Published project accounts are treated as evidence of documented design arrangements, with claims about privacy, care and participation remaining subject to further investigation.

Using precedents as questions for the design

My precedent research focused on single-family and multifamily dwellings, looking closely at their spatial arrangements and how privacy is negotiated within the home. I considered how bedrooms, living areas, entrances and outdoor spaces relate to one another, and how these relationships allow people to share a home while maintaining their own routines.

These studies helped me frame questions to explore within an apartment setting. How can related households live close together and retain independence? How can several generations share a dwelling with space for privacy? I explored these questions through the vertical, horizontal and joint arrangements, considering how connections between homes and spaces within them could support different ways of living.

This also informed my thinking about the occupiable envelope. Balconies, terraces and entrance spaces extend the places available for everyday activities, creating opportunities to spend time together or find space away from shared living areas.

From research to design decisions

Independent homes and changing mobility

BETA’s 3 Generation House in Amsterdam places two family apartments above one another. Its upper apartment includes lift access, level floors and wider door openings in anticipation of reduced mobility. The project demonstrates how vertical proximity can accommodate separate households while access is considered alongside the dwelling arrangement (BETA, n.d., “Adapting with time”; research point 093).

For Together, Apart, I take from this the need to distinguish the family connection from each household’s independent arrival. The S-above-L arrangement combines the private terrace stair with separate front doors reached through shared galleries and a lift. The precedent provides a spatial reference; it does not establish that my proposal achieves equivalent accessibility or privacy.

Outdoor space as part of arriving home

At Veil + Co. House, a covered route connects separate building volumes, while screening addresses shade and overlooking. These elements give the spaces between rooms and dwellings a role in shelter, movement and privacy (Good Design Australia, 2025; project description, “Solution”; research point 094).

I interpret this as a way to consider the apartment edge as usable domestic space. In Together, Apart, deep terraces accommodate seating, planting and everyday activities, while recessed entrances create a transition from the shared gallery to the home. The design question is whether residents can occupy these edges while leaving a clear route past them. The precedent does not establish an appropriate terrace depth for this project; that requires testing through furniture layouts, circulation and environmental conditions.

Shared spaces with a purpose

At Marmalade Lane, individual homes sit within a wider arrangement of shared spaces. A pedestrian street, garden, workshop and common house accommodate movement, gardening, meals and other activities. TOWN’s project account also identifies residents’ involvement in designing and managing the development (TOWN, n.d., “A more neighbourly form of living” and “Collaborative design”; research point 096).

For Together, Apart, this suggests giving shared spaces specific everyday purposes. The laundry, workshop and courtyard offer reasons to leave the apartment and encounter others, while separate homes provide places to withdraw. Galleries must also allow residents to continue home without stopping. These spatial opportunities do not guarantee participation: Marmalade Lane’s collaborative development and management cannot be assumed for the residents of this proposal.
