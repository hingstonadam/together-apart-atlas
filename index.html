The project

Separate homes.
Shared everyday life.

Together, Apart explores how apartment living at Southwark Grounds can support closeness between generations. A grandparent might live one level above family or next door. Each household keeps its own front door and routine, with a stair or balcony making it easier to drop in.

A larger XL dwelling explores how generations could share one home while still having room to withdraw.

Exegesis

From question to proposal

Chapter 01

Position

How can apartment design enable related households to live close together while retaining independence and choosing what they share?

 Chapter 02 La Borda / Lacol. Photo © Lluc Miralles; colour highlights Adam Hingston.

Research

The precedents bring the question of independence into focus at the scale of a room, a household and a shared route.

 Chapter 03

Design approach

DEFINE, EXTEND and MEDIATE organise my approach to domestic independence, outdoor occupation and the relationships between households.

 Chapter 04

Development

The Civic Skin asked how a building edge could be occupied. Housing brought that question down to the depth of a balcony and the position of a door.

 Chapter 05

Proposal

A family can visit by the common walkway or by a more direct connection. The paired homes retain separate arrivals, kitchens and places to withdraw.

 Chapter 06

Reflection

The smaller arrangements made it possible to test everyday routines in detail. The next question is how those relationships carry through the whole building.
